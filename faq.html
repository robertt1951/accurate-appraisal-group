Frequently asked questions

Plain-language answers about how a residential appraisal works and when you need one.

What is a real estate appraisal?

An appraisal is a licensed professional’s independent opinion of a defined type of value — most often market value — as of a specific date. The work is an investigation: inspect the property, gather market and public-record data, apply the approaches to value that fit the assignment, and report the analysis in writing.

How long does it take?

Accurate Appraisal Group’s published standard is an average turn time of 48 hours from the site inspection. Complex properties, limited comparable sales, or delayed access can add time. We confirm timing when the assignment is accepted.

What happens at the inspection?

The appraiser measures the home, notes layout and condition, photographs interior and exterior, and observes amenities that affect value. This is not a home inspection. Make the exterior measurable (trim brush, move obstacles) and provide access to mechanicals such as the furnace and water heater.

What should I have ready?

Helpful items, if you have them: a survey, deed or title report with the legal description, a recent tax bill, a list of personal property included in a sale, and original plans. They are not required for every assignment, but they often improve accuracy and speed.

How is an appraisal different from a home inspection?

A home inspector reports on the condition of systems and structure — roof, electrical, plumbing, heating, foundation. An appraiser forms an opinion of value. The two visits can look similar from the driveway. The products are different, and one does not replace the other.

How is an appraisal different from a CMA?

A comparative market analysis is typically prepared by a real estate agent to help price a listing. An appraisal is prepared by a state-licensed or certified appraiser who must follow professional standards and has no commission tied to the number. Appraisals rely on verified comparable sales and a documented analysis.

What are the three approaches to value?

The sales comparison approach studies recent similar sales and adjusts for differences — the primary method for most houses. The cost approach considers land value plus the cost of improvements less depreciation. The income approach estimates what an investor would pay based on the property’s income and is most relevant for rentals and other income property.

When do people order an appraisal?

Common reasons include a mortgage or refinance, estate or probate administration, divorce, tax questions, insurance replacement considerations, listing or purchase decisions, PMI removal once equity supports it, condemnation matters, and other situations where a supportable value is needed.

What is PMI and how does an appraisal help?

Private mortgage insurance protects the lender when a buyer puts down less than 20 percent. Once you have sufficient equity, a current appraisal is often part of showing the lender that PMI can be removed, which can lower the monthly payment.

Who can use the report?

Appraisal reports identify the client and intended users. A report ordered by a lender is written for that lender. If you need a report for your own decisions — estate, divorce, private sale — say so when you order so the assignment is set up correctly.

Are appraisers licensed in Connecticut?

Yes. Connecticut requires licensing or certification for real estate appraisers. Licensees complete education, experience, and examination requirements and must follow USPAP. Lisa D. Tarver holds Connecticut Certified Residential credential RCR.0002070.
